# SELENE — Cislunar Space Domain Awareness

**Investor and customer briefing. Drafted 2026-10-02.**

SELENE is software that keeps custody of objects between geosynchronous orbit and the Moon (the "xGEO" regime), detects unannounced maneuvers, predicts where a maneuvering object could go, decides where sensors should look next, and lets a program office design the sensor architecture before it buys hardware. It is a defensive awareness and traffic-safety product. It does not do weapons targeting or engagement planning.

## 1. The problem

**The U.S. government has, in the last four years, built a top-to-bottom policy stack that says cislunar space domain awareness is required and does not yet exist.**

- **Executive Order 14369, "Ensuring American Space Superiority" (signed 2025-12-18)** commits the United States to "ensuring the ability to detect, characterize, and counter threats to United States space interests from very low-Earth orbit and through cislunar space" (Sec. 2(b)(ii)), commits to "returning Americans to the Moon by 2028" and "establishing initial elements of a permanent lunar outpost by 2030," and amends Space Policy Directive 3 to remove the "free of direct user fees" language for government space-traffic services — opening the door to paid commercial SSA/STM. [1][2][3]
- **The National Cislunar Science & Technology Strategy (OSTP/NSTC, Nov 2022)** sets four objectives; Objective 3 is "Extend U.S. space situational awareness capabilities into Cislunar space," calling SSA "the necessary foundation to enable transparency and safe operations for all entities operating in Cislunar space." [4]
- **The National Cislunar S&T Action Plan (NSTC, Dec 2024)** assigns DOD, NASA and DOC concrete SSA actions: R&D for "detection, tracking, and characterization of satellites in the Cislunar volume" (3.1.1); evaluating "commercial and international contributors to Cislunar SSA, including both sensors and data processing algorithms" (3.1.2); a study of "satellites in novel orbits, enabled by the gravitational influence of the Moon, to complement existing ground and space-based SSA sensors" (3.2.2); "a publicly available set of astrodynamics routines that enable standardized trajectory uncertainty prediction and propagation" (3.3.2); and an "Integrated Cislunar Object Catalog" (3.4). [5]
- **The Space Force stood up the 19th Space Defense Squadron (Dahlgren, VA) on 2022-04-06** under Space Delta 2 (now Mission Delta 2) with the xGEO remit. Its then-sister-squadron commander said publicly: "As you start to get out past Geosynchronous Orbit, it no longer becomes just a two-body problem." [6][7][89][90] 19 SDS has since supported real lunar missions — maintaining custody of the IM-1 lander "in cislunar space using deep space radars and optical telescopes" and providing launch collision avoidance. [11]
- **In March 2026 the Space Force said it is "serious" about cislunar.** Thomas Ainsworth (identified by Air & Space Forces as Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Space Acquisition and Integration; any acting/"performing the duties of" status is **UNVERIFIED** against an official bio): "We do need to begin integrating cislunar capability into the Space Force. We are serious about that." Gen. Saltzman: "I don't want to get caught flat-footed when we start to have to protect U.S. interests out there." [12][88]
- **In April 2026 the Space Force announced a Cislunar Coordination Office** on the acquisition side, led by Jamie Stearns (spelled "Jamie" by Breaking Defense and ExecutiveGov [13][15], "Jaime" by Air & Space Forces [14]; confirm against an official USSF release), who previously led AFRL's cislunar/Oracle space-control work (described as AFRL Space Vehicles Directorate space-control mission lead by [13][15] and as former Oracle project lead, most recently at Space RCO, by [14]), to build acquisition roadmaps and schedules and to "absolutely be looking to partner with industry." [13][14][15] Gen. Saltzman, as quoted by Breaking Defense: "Let's not underestimate what it takes to do space domain awareness out at cislunar. It is different math and needs different tools to get it right." [13]
- **AFRL's Oracle family of systems** — Oracle-Prime (bound for the Earth–Moon L1 region; spacecraft delivery targeted for 2027, launch date not stated [20]) and Oracle-Mobility — are the first dedicated U.S. cislunar SSA experiments. [19] AFRL's program page says Oracle-P will "detect and track both known and unknown objects in cislunar space" and Oracle-M will "experiment with techniques to maintain custody of objects in this region." [19] Lt. Col. David Johnson, chief of AFRL's space experiments and evaluation division, called the *algorithms* the hard part: "the algorithmic challenges associated with space situational awareness from cislunar space is one of the biggest challenges that we have to overcome." [24]
- **USSPACECOM's "Space Warfighting Environment 2040"** (Aug 2026): "The space between Earth, the Moon, and the Lagrange points contains areas of gravitational stability, maneuver corridors, and vantage points where presence confers positional advantage." [16] SPACECOM's chief scientist: "We want space domain awareness in that area; so, we want to know what our adversary is doing in GEO and beyond." [17]
- **Civil traffic safety has no independent surveillance at all.** JPL's deep-space conjunction-assessment team (MADCAP) writes: "Since there is no space surveillance network for deep space environments, ephemeris sharing is the only way in which spacecraft operators can ensure the safety of their spacecraft from collision in these orbit regimes." [33] Independent astronomer Jonathan McDowell, on lunar traffic: "If you want to know where someone else's lunar spacecraft is, you have to ask them." [34]
- **Traffic is arriving.** Artemis II flew a crewed lunar flyby 2026-04-01 to 2026-04-10. [27] The first crewed landing is targeted for 2028 — CRS (Feb 2026) lists it as Artemis III [28], while NASA's March 2026 $20B lunar-base plan, as reported, describes "at least one and possibly two" landings in 2028 (Artemis 4 and 5) and an aim of crewed landings every six months thereafter [29]; the mission designation and cadence were in flux as of Mar 2026 and should be re-checked against NASA's current manifest. BryceTech (as cited by The Aerospace Corporation) counted 131 uncrewed cislunar missions announced or projected for 2024–2033 (83 U.S., 48 international from 16 countries plus ESA). [35] A 2025 Aerospace Corporation paper cited by Aerospace America projects lunar conjunction incidents could "increase 100-fold" by 2030. [34]

**Yet the government's own documents say today's tooling cannot do the job.** The Action Plan's footnote 7 is the clearest statement: "In the Cislunar regime, motion is better represented by three-body dynamics rather than by two-body dynamics with trajectories represented by Two-Line Elements (TLEs). ... the TLEs are not valid for extended periods. Heritage SSA sensors that only accept tasking in TLE formats will need to be upgraded for Cislunar SSA. Due to the chaotic nature of the dynamic system, small perturbations can cause dramatic changes in Cislunar trajectories, causing SSA systems to require frequent observations, or risk custody of objects in these trajectories." [5] GAO in 2023: "Space Force has limited sensor capability for xGEO objects ... sensors were not designed to collect data beyond GEO." [8]

SELENE is the software layer that closes that gap: a custody loop (orbit determination → maneuver detection → reachability → sensor tasking) built natively for three-body dynamics, plus an architecture studio that tells a program office which sensors, in which orbits, actually buy custody.

## 2. Why it is technically hard — and why incumbents' tooling fails

**2.1 The physics is chaotic, not Keplerian.** Near Earth, motion is dominated by one central body; a six-element TLE and SGP4 are good for days. In xGEO, Earth, Moon and Sun pull comparably and accelerations are "not necessarily directed towards a single central gravitational body." [45] Rosengren (AMOS 2021): xGEO dynamics are "dramatically different than the weakly perturbed Keplerian approach used for over a half century for the detection and tracking of objects near Earth"; he quotes Holzinger et al. that "the ubiquitous two-line element (TLE) will no longer suffice." [40] The same work notes a real science mission's nominal orbit "turned out to be chaotic and unpredictable beyond 2.5 years" from lunar perturbations. [40] Rosengren & Ross (AMOS 2024) map both stable "graveyard" orbits and "chaotic-trajectory regimes that can most easily mask maneuvers." [41]

**2.2 The search volume is enormous.** The Action Plan: "The volume of Cislunar space is more than 2,000 times larger than the entire region of space within geosynchronous orbit, and the farthest region of Cislunar space is more than twelve times the distance of geosynchronous orbit from Earth." [5] (Other sources give ">1,000x" [19][35] or "1,728 times" [12]; the ratio depends on the outer boundary chosen. We quote each with attribution and never blend them.)

**2.3 Observations are sparse and faint.** Range alone costs about seven visual magnitudes: a diffuse 1 m², albedo-0.17 sphere is ">15 visual magnitudes" at GEO but ">22 visual magnitudes" at maximum cislunar range from the ground. [43] Angles-only "too-short-arc" tracks give poor in-track velocity information, so initial orbit determination is hard. [43] Holzinger told the Senate in 2021: "our core capabilities in observation association and initial orbit determination often break down in multi-body regions ... we're asking our existing algorithms to perform tasks they were never designed for." [42] A 2026 preprint shows "a key assumption of Gauss's method is rendered incompatible" in cislunar three-body dynamics and proposes particle-cloud IOD instead. [47]

**2.4 Sun and Moon exclusion blind the sensors.** AFRL's own photometric simulations use exclusion zones of 20° from the Sun's limb ("This also results in sensor saturation"), 5° from Earth, 5° from the Moon, and conclude "the observability of cislunar objects is more greatly affected by lunar and solar exclusion zones than when objects are in orbits at or inside GEO"; an L2 halo object has "a large gap at the full Moon." [44] Trusted Space: "Low SNR due to vast distances and lunar exclusion zones prevent existing space tracking systems from providing custody of cislunar objects." [43] Even notional space-based constellations are reported to remain limited by lunar occlusion and albedo. [46] (abstract-only; wording to be confirmed on the DOI landing page)

**2.5 Uncertainty is non-Gaussian, so standard filters break.** Space Force SBIR language: "initial uncertainties in xGEO orbits are therefore highly non-Gaussian, which inhibits the effectiveness of traditional propagation and filtering methods." [10] Academic literature agrees: sparse observations plus chaotic dynamics produce highly non-Gaussian state distributions. [46] (abstract-only; wording to be confirmed on the DOI landing page)

**2.6 Why incumbents' tooling fails.** Legacy catalog and conjunction tools are built around two-body special-perturbation catalogs of Earth orbit (e.g., the Astrodynamics Support Workstation, operational since 1998, describes no cislunar capability — **UNVERIFIED** [97]). Commercial SSA suites that list "cislunar" as a supported regime [54] still center on Earth-orbit catalogs, EKF-class filters, and TLE/ephemeris ingest. General-purpose astrodynamics tools (STK, FreeFlyer) are used for cislunar transfer design and station-keeping [59] (native three-body propagation and OD capability is widely understood but **UNVERIFIED** against the product documentation we could read [60]) but are analyst desktops, not an operational custody loop with tasking and architecture scoring. The government knows this: SBIR topics SF222-D009 (2023, $1.24M to Trusted Space: probabilistic admissible-region IOD and particle Gaussian-mixture filtering for three-body dynamics) and SF242-D022 (CUSTOD-E, $1.80M to Advanced Space) are the Space Force buying exactly these algorithms. [9][10]

## 6. The moat

**6.1 Native cislunar dynamics.** SELENE's core is the circular restricted three-body problem (CR3BP) in the Earth–Moon rotating frame plus a higher-fidelity ephemeris model (Earth, Moon and Sun point masses from JPL DE440s, optional solar radiation pressure), with exact frame transforms between rotating, Earth-centered inertial (GCRF) and Moon-centered frames. On top of that sits a computed periodic-orbit library — L1/L2 Lyapunov and halo families, the 9:2 near-rectilinear halo orbit, distant retrograde orbits, DROs and resonant orbits — produced by differential correction and continuation — plus a Keplerian-seeded elliptical lunar frozen orbit (Ely 2005) propagated under the ephemeris model, with stability indices, and checked against published initial conditions (Koon–Lo–Marsden–Ross; Richardson; Zimovan-Spreen/Howell/Davis; NASA Gateway NRHO documentation) as cited in the code. This is the "different math" [13] the Space Force says it needs, implemented once and reused by every downstream engine.

**6.2 Estimation built for sparse, non-Gaussian, chaotic tracking.** Angles-only initial orbit determination adapted to three-body dynamics, batch least squares and an unscented Kalman filter running on the ephemeris model, particle-cloud propagation to show custody decay honestly, and covariance-realism monitoring (NEES/NIS) so operators can trust the numbers. Maneuver detection uses residual, Mahalanobis and filter-consistency tests with a *configurable* false-alarm rate, and estimates Δv magnitude, direction and epoch.

**6.3 Information-theoretic sensor tasking.** Given a heterogeneous network (ground telescopes with Sun/Moon/Earth exclusion, eclipse, elevation, field-of-view and slew constraints; space-based observers in GEO, halo or DRO), SELENE schedules observations to maximize expected information gain (log-determinant covariance reduction), reports custody percentage and mean time-since-last-observation per object, and compares greedy scheduling against an optimization baseline. Reachability sets (sampled Δv over 24–168 h) tell the tasker *where* to look when an object goes dark.

**6.4 Architecture design tool.** The same engines run in Monte Carlo over candidate architectures — sensors placed in GEO, L1/L2 halos, DROs, resonant orbits — scoring coverage %, custody %, revisit time and maneuver-detection latency. This is precisely the study the Action Plan assigns to DOD/NASA (3.2.2) [5] and that the Cislunar Coordination Office must produce to build roadmaps [14]. It is the sales tool for government architecture studies and for primes writing proposals.

**6.5 What the MVP in this repository demonstrates.** PLAN.md defines eleven milestones with acceptance criteria; PROGRESS.md records which are done. The module set is: `dynamics` (CR3BP, DE440s ephemeris, frames, propagation), `orbits` (shooting, continuation, families, stability, cached library), `objects` (SIMULATED notional catalog and a JPL Horizons client for real published cislunar spacecraft), `sensors` (sites, space observers, photometry, constraints, visibility, coverage heatmap), `od` (measurements, IOD, batch, UKF, particles, realism), `maneuver` (detection, Δv estimation), `reachability` (sampling, named high-value regions), `tasking` (information gain, greedy, optimization, metrics), `architecture` (candidates, Monte Carlo), `scenario` (scripted two-minute demo and auto-generated analyst brief), and a FastAPI/React ops-center UI with a 3D Earth–Moon scene, rotating/inertial frame toggle, uncertainty clouds, sensor fields of view and exclusion cones, a timeline and events feed. Acceptance tests include Jacobi-constant conservation, periodic-orbit closure, literature checks on orbit periods, NEES within chi-square bounds, false-alarm rate matching the configured α, and reproducible Monte Carlo scores. The demo story: a SIMULATED object of a notional actor sits in a DRO, performs an unannounced burn, is lost by ground telescopes in lunar glare, its uncertainty cloud balloons, reachability shows it could reach the L1 gateway corridor near a notional allied relay in NRHO, the tasker redirects a DRO-based observer, custody is regained, the maneuver is characterized, and a plain-English analyst brief is generated. Everything runs offline from cached DE440s and Horizons data with one command.

**6.6 Why this compounds.** Each engine is a reusable, tested module; the orbit library, coverage grids and demo scenarios are precomputed and cached; the entire stack is sensor-agnostic. As real observation feeds and flight data arrive (§8), the same code calibrates against truth and the architecture studio's Monte Carlo becomes evidence a program office can cite. The defensibility is the integration and the validated physics, not any single algorithm — many of which are published.

## 9. Risks and honest limitations

**Market and program risk.**
- Cislunar remains pre-program-of-record. The Coordination Office is building roadmaps; Air & Space Forces reported in April 2026 that "the Space Force hasn't dedicated significant funding to these efforts" (the reporter's assessment, not a quoted official). [14] No cislunar budget line has been identified; the only published dollar figure is a think-tank estimate. [87]
- Schedules slip. Oracle moved from a 2025 to a 2027 launch because "the original schedule was too ambitious." [22] Gateway is "paused in its current form." [29] CLPS has had failures (Peregrine did not land; IM-2 tipped over; ispace crashed). [36][37] Fewer missions means less urgency.
- Big SDA money (Andromeda) is explicitly GEO-focused today. [56] SELENE's defense revenue in the near term is SBIR-scale.
- Incumbent data owners (Anduril/ExoAnalytic, Slingshot) or Advanced Space could productize a competing software stack; primes could build in-house.

**Technical limitations of the current MVP (stated plainly).**
- The MVP is at the milestone recorded in PROGRESS.md; capabilities not marked done there are planned, not demonstrated.
- All objects and maneuvers in the demo are SIMULATED. Custody and detection metrics are computed on synthetic observations generated by our own sensor models; real-data performance is unknown until Phase B.
- The ephemeris model is point-mass Earth/Moon/Sun with optional SRP; it omits Earth and lunar gravity-field harmonics, planetary perturbations and thermal forces that matter for precision OD near the Moon.
- Photometry assumes a Lambertian sphere with a configured albedo; real objects have attitude-dependent light curves and glints.
- The angles-only IOD and UKF are Gaussian-core methods with particle augmentation; a full particle-Gaussian-mixture filter (as in [9][46][47]) is future work. Expect degraded performance on very short arcs and after large unobserved maneuvers.
- Sensor models do not include detector-level noise, atmospheric seeing, weather outage statistics, or real telescope scheduling constraints; coverage heatmaps are therefore optimistic.
- Reachability sampling is impulsive-Δv only; low-thrust spiral maneuvers are not modeled.
- Custody will often be poor in honest runs. That is the market problem; SELENE reports it rather than hiding it.

**Regulatory and operational risk.**
- Export controls: SSA analysis software may fall under ITAR Category XV or EAR 9D515 depending on whether it is "specially designed" for a defense article; BIS proposed a new ECCN 9D515.c for "Space Situational Awareness (SSA) analysis software" in Oct 2024 (finalization status **UNVERIFIED**). [83] Foreign-national hiring and allied sales depend on a formal classification obtained early.
- SBIR eligibility now requires disclosure of foreign-country-of-concern ties including venture backing and IP licences; cap-table hygiene is a gating item. [69]
- Accreditation timelines are long and outside our control. [84]
- Any perception that the product supports targeting would be fatal to the civil channel; the product boundary (awareness and safety only) must be enforced in code, documentation and contracts.
